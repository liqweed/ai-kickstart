# 01 · Component inventory: ai-kickstart.me

What the site must make possible, framed as **problems to solve**. How many pages there are, how they're split, what they're called and how they're navigated is open (`04 §3.1`).

## Must be able to…

### Explain the idea in one breath
- Say what this is, who it's for and why it's different from "just chatting with AI". **Problem:** visitors arrive from a friend's link with near-zero context and possibly some AI scepticism. The answer must land before they scroll.
- Convey the **four pieces** (vault you own · interview · harness plan · skills & prompts) and how they fit, without the reader needing to know what Obsidian, a vault or an agent is.

### Help the visitor choose how to start
- Lay out the **ways in**: download the kit (a zip) · read the guide online · *(later)* download the companion app. Plus the three **AI paths** (chat app / desktop agent / terminal agent) and the visitor's OS (macOS / Windows / Linux / phone).
- **Problem:** that's a lot of branching for a nervous beginner. Every visitor should end up on exactly one clear next action that fits them.

### Earn trust
- Show that it's free, open source, local-first (your notes stay on your computer), with no account, and who made it.
- **Problem:** trust signals must be legible to a non-technical person ("open source" means little to them). The site is also asking them to download something.

### Walk through setup
- Setup takes about an hour, with steps per OS and per AI path, and includes copy-paste prompts.
- **Problem:** during setup the visitor is switching between this site, Obsidian and an AI app, often on a small laptop screen or with the site open on their phone. The current step and its prompt must be findable again instantly.

### Teach the system (for the curious)
- The rationale and principles, the layers (vault, profile, map & rules, skills, connections, scripts, automations, harness plan), and "who owns what" (code owns rules & effects, AI owns judgment & drafts, you own decisions).
- **Problem:** depth for those who want it, invisible for those who don't.

### Show the interview
- Five rounds, with time and output per round, and how each round feeds the next. Includes the prompts to start each round, plus express and chat-only variants.

### Let people explore the catalog
- 103 functions in 16 areas, each with a level (S Starter / M Connected / L Builder). Visitors tick what appeals and copy their picks as a ready-made message for interview Round 2. Picks persist locally (browser storage).
- **Problem:** 103 items invite overwhelm. Picking should feel like browsing a menu, not filling in a form.

### Explain the harness plan
- Phases P0–P7, the anatomy of a step ("Done when" + "Check"), status marks, and the one sentence to remember: **"Continue my harness plan."**
- **Problem:** "progress over months, at your own pace" has to feel encouraging, not like homework.

### Serve the prompt library
- About 40 prompts in 6 groups (setup, interview, harness, everyday life, work, making it better), each with one-click copy. Placeholders like `<Name>` must be obvious.

### Welcome returning visitors
- What's new in the latest version (changelog), and how to update an existing vault without losing notes *(the kit doesn't fully solve this yet, so the design can show where it would live)*.

### Footer-level essentials
- Credit line, licence (CC BY 4.0), link to the GitHub repo, version number, "give feedback" (GitHub issue templates exist).

## Already-canonical primitives (binding: reuse, don't reinvent)

None. The current guide's components are a reference, not a constraint.
