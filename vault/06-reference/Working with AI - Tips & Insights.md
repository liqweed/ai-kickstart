# Working with AI: tips & insights

These come from a lot of real, daily use across family life, home admin and work. None are rules. Try two or three, keep what sticks, and come back in a month. Add your own at the bottom.

> [!tip] If you read nothing else: the top 10
> 1. **Talk, don't type.** Dictate long, messy answers. Turning them into structure is the AI's job.
> 2. **Ask it to interview you** before any non-trivial task: *"What would you need to know to do this really well? Ask me one question at a time."*
> 3. **Teach it once.** When you correct it, say *"remember that"* so the fix lands in your preferences or a skill, not just this chat.
> 4. **Three options, one recommendation.** Ask for decisions, not catalogues.
> 5. **AI drafts, you send.** Money, messages to people and anything irreversible go through you.
> 6. **Check anything that matters.** Dates, prices, opening hours, laws and numbers: ask for sources, or have it calculate rather than recall.
> 7. **"It ran" isn't "it worked".** Look for the result, not the "done".
> 8. **One task, one session.** Start fresh for new topics. Long chats get muddled.
> 9. **Finish before adding.** One thing you use beats five you set up.
> 10. **The weekly review is the heartbeat.** Skip it for a month and the system quietly dies. Keep it, and everything else stays alive.

## 🌱 Habits that make it stick

- **Twenty minutes is the unit of progress.** Don't plan a weekend overhaul. "Continue my harness plan" when you have a gap is enough.
- **Pick a fixed slot for the weekly review**, like Sunday evening with tea. A habit beats a reminder.
- **Capture everywhere, sort weekly.** Phone, dictation, screenshot, whatever's fastest goes into `_inbox/`. Don't organise in the moment.
- **Keep a tiny daily floor, and never let it grow.** For anything you're learning (a language, an instrument, fitness), agree with the AI on a small daily minimum and keep it small. Make the weekly *refills* bigger, not the daily ask. Small daily wins beat ambitious plans you abandon.
- **Close loops before adding capability.** When you're excited to build something new, first ask: *"What did I start that isn't finished or isn't used?"*
- **Do a monthly honesty check:** what did I build that I never use? Archive it without guilt.

## 💬 Asking well

- **Say who it's for and why.** *"Explain this to my 12-year-old"* and *"Summarise this for my boss, who has 30 seconds"* produce very different, much better answers.
- **Show an example of "good".** Paste a previous email you liked, a format you want, a past plan. One example beats a paragraph of description.
- **Name the constraints up front:** budget, time, diet, language, "must be bookable online", "nothing after 22:00". Constraints are what make an answer *yours*.
- **Ask for the shape you'll use:** a checklist you can tick, a table to compare, a message you can paste, a calendar-ready plan.
- **Use "what am I missing?"** at the end of planning anything. It's the cheapest insurance there is.
- **Ask it to argue the other side.** AI tends to agree with you. Try *"Make the strongest case against this"* or *"Red-team my plan."*
- **Turn repeats into skills.** If you've typed roughly the same request twice, say *"make this a skill"*.

## 🔎 Trust, but verify

- **AI is confident even when it's wrong.** Treat facts it hasn't looked up as *probably right, unverified*: prices, opening times, legal deadlines, medical dosages, dates.
- **"Unknown" is a good answer.** Praise it when it says "I couldn't confirm". Push back when it fills gaps with guesses.
- **Make it show its work:** *"List the claims you made and how you verified each."*
- **Numbers belong in code or a spreadsheet.** For anything with sums, dates or filters, ask the AI to calculate with a tool or script, not "in its head".
- **Links can be invented.** Click before you rely on one.
- **Get a second opinion on big decisions.** A fresh session, or a different AI, reviewing the same plan catches a surprising amount.
- **Automations need a check on the output.** A scheduled job that "succeeded" can still have produced nothing. Look for the file, the email or the note.

## 🧠 Keeping context healthy

- **Fresh session per task.** Very long conversations drift and forget early details. End with a short handoff ("save the summary to my notes") and start clean.
- **Search before you create.** Ask the AI to update an existing note rather than making a new one. Two notes on one topic is how a vault rots.
- **Keep always-read files short.** `AGENTS.md` and `PREFERENCES.md` are read every session. Every line costs attention. When the AI makes the same mistake twice, fix the skill or the folder structure first, and add a rule only as a last resort.
- **Put a small index in big folders.** A short `_index.md` saying "what's in here and where things go" saves the AI from guessing, whether in your vault or a shared drive.
- **A one-line `summary` on every note is the best investment in your vault.** It lets the AI (and you) decide what to open without opening everything. More in [[Organizing your vault]].
- **A monthly health check keeps the rot out.** Broken links, duplicates and an outdated index make the AI guess. *"Vault health check"* takes 15 minutes.
- **Private vs. shareable versions.** Keep your honest, private analysis in your vault. When something goes to a shared document, ask for a neutral version. Never paste your private angle into a shared doc by accident.

## 🔒 Privacy & safety

- **Write as if the AI provider can read it,** because it processes what it reads. No passwords, PINs, ID or full account numbers, ever. Use a password manager.
- **Turn off "use my data for training"** in your AI provider's settings.
- **Give agents one folder,** not your whole computer. Give connections read-only access first, then drafts, and "act" only for small, reversible things.
- **Web pages, emails and PDFs can contain hidden instructions** aimed at AI ("ignore previous instructions and…"). Your vault rules tell the AI to treat content as data. Still, be wary when an AI suddenly wants to do something you didn't ask for.
- **Work and AI:** follow your employer's AI policy. Keep their confidential documents out of your personal setup.
- **Other people's information:** write about people the way you'd be fine with them reading it.

## 🏡 Life admin

- **Every official letter hides a deadline.** Ask: *"What do I need to do, by when, and what happens if I don't?"* Then put a dated task in your calendar or to-do app **the same week**.
- **Endings have paperwork clocks.** When something ends (a contract, a job, a lease, a policy, sadly sometimes a life), notice periods and claim windows start running. Ask the AI for an "ending checklist" right away. Missed windows quietly cost money.
- **Use AI to prepare for professionals, not to replace them.** Before the doctor, accountant or lawyer, have it explain your situation in plain words and list your questions. You'll get far more from the appointment.
- **Subscriptions creep.** Do a subscriptions audit every quarter, including your AI subscriptions.
- **Keep a home manual.** Appliance models, filter sizes, warranty dates, who fixed what. The next breakdown becomes a five-minute fix instead of an afternoon.

## 👨‍👩‍👧 Family & people

- **Hard constraints are rules, not preferences.** Allergies, diets, accessibility, language and bedtimes go at the top of people profiles. Ask the AI to *check* them, not assume them.
- **Use people's chosen names**, and keep profiles kind and factual.
- **Kids and AI:** use it to explain differently and to quiz, not to do the homework. *"Quiz me, and don't give me the answer until I've tried"* works wonders.
- **Plan together.** Share the AI's three options with your partner or family and decide together. It works much better than presenting a finished plan.

## 💼 Work (any job)

- **Keep a wins log.** One line whenever something goes well. Review season becomes copy-paste.
- **Meeting notes → actions immediately.** Paste rough notes and get decisions, owners, dates and a follow-up email in two minutes.
- **Rehearse hard conversations.** Ask the AI to play the other person, realistically and not too nicely.
- **Draft in your voice.** Give it two or three emails you wrote and liked as examples.
- **Prefer official connections over clicking around.** Where an app has a proper connector or command-line tool, use it. Having the AI drive a web page is slower and breaks more often.
- **Connections multiply.** Calendar plus maps gives you "leave by" times. Email plus tasks turns every deadline into a to-do. Ask *"What could I connect?"* once you're past the basics.

## ⚙️ When you get more technical

- **Script lists, search questions.** If a list must be filtered correctly every time, have the AI write a small script. If it's an open question, ask it.
- **Tools need tests, and connections need notes.** A script without a test breaks silently. A connection without a note is forgotten, along with its permissions. `TOOLS-CONTRACT.md` has the short checklist for both.
- **Never combine all three:** private data, untrusted content (web, email) and a way to send things out, in one unsupervised agent. That's how data gets leaked by a hidden instruction.
- **Keep your vault in version control (git) or synced with history.** Then any change the AI makes can be undone.
- **Let agents work on a copy.** For bigger changes, have the AI work on a branch or a copy and review the result before it replaces the original.
- **Use the right model for the job.** The most capable model for planning, tricky reasoning and reviews. Faster, cheaper ones for routine summaries and sorting.
- **Keep your computer's startup scripts fast and quiet.** Agents run commands constantly, and anything slow or interactive at shell start makes every step slower or makes it hang.

## ⚠️ Common pitfalls

| Pitfall | Better |
|---|---|
| Building ten things in week one | Three first wins, used twice each |
| Re-explaining yourself every chat | Profile files + "remember that" |
| Accepting the first answer | "What am I missing?" / "Argue the other side" |
| Trusting a confident date or price | Ask for the source, or check it yourself |
| One endless conversation | Fresh session per task, with a saved handoff |
| Duplicate notes everywhere | Search first, update existing notes |
| An automation nobody checks | Verify the output for two weeks |
| Long rulebooks for the AI | Short rules. Fix skills and structure instead |
| Letting AI send or buy | AI drafts, you decide and send |
