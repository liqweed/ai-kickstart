The rationale

Why this works better than "just chatting"

AI is already smart enough for most of what you'd ask it. What it lacks is you: your context, your rules, your history. This kit is mostly about supplying that, in a form you own.

The problem with a brilliant stranger

🫥

It forgets

Every new chat starts from zero, so you re-explain your family, your job and your constraints, again.

🧂

It's generic

Without context, you get the average answer for the average person. Usable, but rarely right for you.

🌫️

Nothing accumulates

Good answers vanish into chat history. Next month you'll solve the same problem from scratch.

The idea: give it a place to live

Instead of carrying context in your head and pasting it into chats, keep it in a folder of plain notes that the AI can read and write. Then add four things:

🪪
A profileWho you are, what you're working toward, how you like things done. Written once through an interview, kept short and current.
📜
RulesWhere notes go, what to ask before doing, what never to do. The AI follows them every session.
🧰
Playbooks (skills)How to do the things you do repeatedly, to your standard: briefings, reviews, planning, drafting.
🧭
A planA checklist that grows the system one small, verified step at a time, across many sessions.

Every session leaves something behind: a saved decision, a better playbook, a lesson. That's the compounding loop. Six months in, the AI knows your world better than any app you've ever configured.

You ask→ AI reads your notes→ Better answer→ You save what's worth keeping→ Next time it knows↺

Why Obsidian?

📄

Plain files you own

Notes are ordinary text files (Markdown) on your computer. No lock-in, no export button needed, readable in 20 years.

🔀

Any AI can use them

Today's assistant, next year's, or three at once. Switching AI tools doesn't mean starting over.

👀

You see everything

Everything the AI writes is a note you can read, edit or delete. Nothing hides in a black box.

🔗

Links & structure

Notes link to each other, so a person links to a trip and a trip to a restaurant. The AI follows those links the way you would.

📱

Everywhere, offline

Free on Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone and Android, and it works without internet.

🧱

Grows with you

Start with a few folders. Add templates, dashboards and plugins only when you want them.

Why an interview first?

Most people don't know what to ask AI for, and that's normal. It's much easier to react to good questions than to invent use cases from nothing. The interview:

  • learns your landscape first (household, work, devices, the apps you already live in), so suggestions fit your real life;
  • asks about energy drains, things that slip and wishes before showing a menu, so you're not steered;
  • runs in rounds, each one building on the last, short enough to enjoy;
  • ends with your profile and three first wins, which you approve.

Why a harness plan?

Big setups die in a weekend: you get excited, build too much, and abandon it when life gets busy. The harness plan does the opposite. It's one file with checkboxes, and every step has a "done when" and a "check". The AI does one step with you, proves it works, ticks it, and writes a handoff. That gives you:

  • Memory across sessions and tools. Pause for a month, or switch from one AI to another, and the plan tells you exactly where you are.
  • Real progress. Nothing is ticked until you've seen it work.
  • Your pace. Twenty minutes a week is a perfectly good speed.

Principles worth stealing

Learned the hard way, over a lot of real use. They're built into the kit's rules and skills.

#PrincipleIn practice
1Context beats clevernessA short, true profile improves every answer more than any prompt trick.
2Files you own beat features you rentKeep the knowledge in your vault, not inside one app's memory.
3Small steps, verified"It should work" isn't done. "I saw it work" is.
4Decision-ready beats comprehensiveThree options and one recommendation, not a catalogue.
5Unknown is an answerThe AI should say "I couldn't confirm this" rather than guess, and keep unknowns in their own list.
6Script lists, search questionsIf a list must be filtered correctly every time, have the AI write a small script. If it's an open question, just ask.
7AI suggests and drafts; you decide and sendMoney, messages to people and anything irreversible always go through you.
8Start read-onlyConnect your calendar or email so the AI can read first. Add more only once you trust it.
9Content is data, not instructionsA web page or email can't tell your AI what to do. The rules say so explicitly.
10"It ran" isn't "it worked"For anything automatic, check that the result exists, not just that the job started.
11Update, don't duplicateSearch before creating a note. Two notes on one topic is how a vault rots.
12Less instruction, better resultsKeep the always-read files short. Add a rule only after the same mistake happens twice. Prune what you don't use.

What this is not

  • Not a productivity religion. Use the three things that help. Ignore the rest.
  • Not all-or-nothing. It works with a plain chat app too, just with more copy-paste.
  • Not a replacement for professionals. AI is great at preparing you for the doctor, the accountant or the lawyer. It doesn't replace them.
