How it's built

The system, layer by layer

Eight layers, each one a few plain files. You start with the top four and add the rest when they'd help. Nothing here needs code until you want it to.

The layers

🗂️
VaultYour notes, in folders by area of life. Obsidian is the window; the files are yours. 01-life/ … 07-briefings/
🪪
ProfileSeven short files about you: mission, goals, projects, landscape, preferences, beliefs, lessons. _system/profile/
🗺️
Map & rulesThe index (what lives where) and the contract (how to write). Read at the start of every session. _system/index.md, VAULT-CONTRACT.md
🧰
SkillsPlaybooks for things you do repeatedly. When a request matches one, the AI follows it. _system/skills/<name>/SKILL.md
🔌
ConnectionsRead access to your calendar, tasks, email or school portal. Added one at a time, read-only first. 06-reference/connections/
⚙️
ScriptsSmall programs for jobs with one right answer: filtering, renaming, calculating. The AI writes them with you. skills/<name>/scripts/
⏰
AutomationsThings that run on a schedule: Sunday review prep, a morning digest. Checked by their output, not their "ok".
🧭
Harness planThe checklist that grows all of the above, one verified step at a time. _system/HARNESS-PLAN.md

Who owns what

The single most useful design rule: give each kind of work to whoever is reliably good at it.

⚙️

Code owns rules & effects

Dates, filters, calculations, exact lists, file moves, anything that must be right every time. And the actual doing: sending, booking, paying.

✨

AI owns judgment & drafts

Understanding messy input, ranking options, summarising, drafting, explaining, spotting what you missed. It proposes, abstains, or says "unknown".

🙋

You own decisions

What matters, what's true about your life, and anything irreversible: money, messages to people, deleting things.

What happens in a session

Open AI in the vault→ Reads AGENTS.md, index, preferences→ Matches a skill (or just helps)→ Does the work→ Checks it→ Saves what's worth keeping

Notice what it doesn't do: read your whole vault. The index tells it where to look, so it opens only the 3–8 notes the task needs. That keeps answers fast, cheap and focused.

Anatomy of a good skill

The quality-time-planner skill is included as a worked example. It's a generalised version of a date-night planner that's been used for real evenings out. These are its patterns, and they transfer to any skill you build:

🎯

Trigger phrases

The description lists what you'd actually say ("date night", "what should we watch"), so the AI knows when to use it.

📝

First-run interview

Asks 5–6 setup questions once and saves the answers to config/. You can edit them anytime.

🚧

Gates before ranking

Hard constraints (diet, language, budget, home-by time) filter first, and are checked from data, never assumed.

❓

Unknown is its own list

"Couldn't confirm subtitles" goes in a separate verify before booking list, never mixed in with the confirmed picks.

🥇

Three options, one pick

Ranked by clear signals (already on your wishlist, fits the person, quality, novelty), with one line on why.

🤝

Offer, don't act

Saving the plan, setting a reminder and logging how it went are all offered. Booking always stays with you.

Script it or search it? If a list must be filtered correctly every time (e.g. a cinema schedule by time window and language), the AI should write a small script. If it's an open question (what's good this weekend, where to eat), it should search. That one rule keeps your skills both reliable and flexible.
